# Modeling Like Publix at 6 Employees — Plan + ERP Implications

Reference: Publix is ~240k employee-owners (ESOP). We are ~6 people. We should
adopt the *principles* (employee ownership, financial transparency, financial
literacy, profit sharing) in a form scaled to a tiny team — not the ESOP
machinery.

## 1. What to adopt (scaled)
- **Ownership in spirit, not an ESOP.** A 6-person ESOP/qualified plan is heavy
  legal/administrative overhead. Alternatives that fit:
  - **Profit-sharing pool** (simplest): a set % of net profit distributed
    quarterly/annually by tenure/role/performance.
  - **Phantom equity / unit plan:** book-value units that pay out on exit or
    defined milestones; simple grant agreement, no SEC/ERISA plan.
  - **Real buy-in equity** only if owners genuinely want co-owners; requires an
    operating agreement + valuation process.
- **Open books:** every employee can see real revenue, COGS, labor, margin,
  channel and product profitability (we already produce most of this).
- **Financial literacy:** spend the time teaching the 6 how to read the numbers
  (Publix runs continuous financial-education courses; we can do monthly
  "numbers lunch").
- **Transparent comp policy:** clear pay bands / how profit share is computed.

## 2. Step-by-step rollout
1. **Agree on principles + metric set.** Decide which numbers are open to all
   (revenue, margin, labor, profit) vs. kept private (individual salaries, if
   any).
2. **Choose the ownership vehicle** — recommend starting with a profit-sharing
   pool + optional phantom equity; defer a real ESOP.
3. **Define the sharing formula** (e.g., 10–20% of net profit, split by base +
   performance/tenure).
4. **Publish open reporting** monthly (see ERP section).
5. **Educate:** monthly walkthroughs so the 6 can interpret the books.
6. **Review cadence + docs:** operate a simple agreement documenting the pool,
   payout schedule, and buyout/vesting rules.

## 3. ERP perspective (what the system must support)
We already have the raw data; the ask is presentation + distribution accounting.

- **Open, permissioned dashboards.** Expose a clean P&L to employees: revenue,
  COGS, gross margin, labor cost, net profit — broken out by source (Shopify vs
  Square) and channel (POS vs online) and product. This is the "open books"
  surface.
- **Profit-center accounting.** Attribute costs to channels/products so the
  shared profit number is credible and nobody feels it's manipulated.
- **Labor + payroll integration.** Compute labor cost per period and feed it
  into the P&L; support the profit-share calculation (linked to payroll records).
- **Equity/distribution ledger.** Track profit-share accruals, payouts, and (if
  phantom equity) units/vesting/buyout — a small "equity & distributions" module.
- **Forecast vs. actual.** Publix-style transparency works when the number is
  trusted; add budget/forecast so employees see plan vs. actual, not just
  hindsight.
- **Read-only reporting for the team.** Reuse the existing quick reports /
  sales_report to give everyone self-serve access to the open numbers.

## 4. Risks to plan around
- **ESOP complexity at our size** — avoid; use profit-sharing/phantom equity.
- **Misreading the numbers** — solve with mandatory financial-literacy sessions.
- **Individual comp privacy** — keep salaries private unless the team opts in;
  transparency ≠ publishing everyone's pay.
- **Trust** — the model fails if the numbers look massaged; keep the P&L
  auditable and tie profit share mechanically to the same ledger we use to run
  the business.

## 5. Recommendation
Start with: (1) open monthly P&L dashboards across the team, (2) a
profit-sharing pool of ~10–15% of net profit, (3) monthly numbers-education,
(4) a lightweight equity/distribution ledger in the ERP. Revisit a formal ESOP
only if/when headcount and legal appetite grow.
